# Changelog

Format based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), versions follow [SemVer](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- **The graphics are realistic** (ADR 0015, spec 13 v3): the whole art of the game is painted again to look real, not drawn.
  - **The ground** has the matter of real ground: grass made of blades in clumps with their contact shadow, grit and pebbles on the
    paths, ripples on the sand, leaf litter in the forests, cushions of moss, the net of light on the bottom of the shallows and long
    ripples on deep water, ember cracks in the ash, cobbles worn round, boards with their grain, plaster and stone with their roughness.
  - **Trees** are a mass of leaves (leaves on the edge of the crown, dark gaps, leaves picked out by the light) on trunks of furrowed
    bark; pines have ragged boughs; rocks have cracks, pits, lichen and moss.
  - **Every object, person and creature** loses its cartoon outline and its hard shade: soft light from the top left, a volume, a fine
    grain, colours a little less vivid. People have the face of a real face (smaller almond eyes, a nose, lips), strands in their hair
    and a weave in their clothes; beasts have fur, armours scratches and rust.
  - **The rasterizer** of both clients gains stretched noise (`scale_y`, `period_y`: fibres, blades, strands, ripples) and ridged noise
    (`ridged`: cracks, veins, caustics), with conformance probes of their own; old recipes draw as before.
- **The desktop window opens maximized**, at the full resolution of the screen (spec 16, "Desktop"); it can still be resized.

### Added

- **The duo strike** (spec 10, "Duo strike"): once the bond with a companion reaches twenty kills, a power strike near it brings its
  hit too, three times its attack, on the first creature the strike hits, every ten seconds (the strays of the party: two and a half
  times). Same rule in the reference, the vectors (scenario `companion_duo`) and both clients.

- **The world ahead is made on a thread** (item C4.1, spec 09 "Streaming"): both clients generate the ring of chunks just outside the
  window on a thread of their own, and the window takes them when they are ready, so walking into new land costs the frame almost
  nothing. The simulation still loads the window at the same point of the tick: nothing of the game changes, and a test of each client
  compares a store with the thread and one without, chunk by chunk. A nightly job runs that test of the desktop under ThreadSanitizer.
- **The layout of the touch buttons** (item E2.1, spec 16 "The layout of the buttons"): on Android, the Dodge, Strike, Parry and Analyze
  buttons can be moved and resized. `Button layout`, at the top right of the open journal, opens the editor: drag a button to move it,
  `-` and `+` change its size, `Reset` puts every button back, `Done` keeps the layout. The layout survives a restart and a turn of the
  phone (it is kept in permille of the screen).
- **The hints follow the last device used** (spec 16, "The last device"): a keyboard shows the keys, a touch screen the buttons and a
  controller its buttons; nine hints have a text of their own for a controller, in Italian and English.
- **More of the controller**: `Start` opens the bag and `Back` the journal, on the desktop and on Android; in the journal the D-pad
  scrolls and `B` or `Back` closes it.

- **The quality of the graphics** (spec 13, "Quality"; item E1.1): low, normal or high, kept in the settings. Low bakes the textures
  at most at 64 pixels a tile and leaves out the moving water, the rounded corners of the ground and the long shadows of dawn and dusk;
  normal bakes them at most at 128; high at 256, as before. Desktop: the last row of the settings panel (`F10`). Android: a button at
  the bottom of the open journal. The first time, the game chooses: the desktop high, Android from the memory the phone gives it. A test
  in the reference and in both clients checks that all the art fits the memory budget of each level.
- **A controller on Android** (spec 16; item E2.2): the first connected game controller plays with the buttons of the gamepad of the
  desktop. The left stick moves; `A` attacks and confirms; `X` talks and confirms; `RB` is the power strike, `LB` the parry; `B` rolls
  and closes a panel; `Y` switches buy and sell in a shop; the D-pad moves in lists and choices. Every panel works with it as with a
  keyboard.
- **The release pipeline** (item E3.1): on a tag `v*` the release workflow builds every package with the same workflows as the pull
  requests, names them after the version, writes `SHA256SUMS` and a bill of materials (SPDX, `tools/licenses.py --sbom`), checks the
  sums and creates a draft release with all the files; it never publishes. By hand it makes a dry run, with the files as an artifact.
- **Measures** (item C4.2; `docs/design/performance.md`): a performance test in both clients with wide budgets for a chunk of the world
  (25 ms) and a tick (4 ms), whose numbers the CI prints; a nightly workflow that runs the game on an Android emulator of API 30 and 34
  (start, rotation, pause and resume, the same game after them).

### Fixed

- **Android after a lost graphics context**: the textures of the art and of the font are now managed by libGDX, which loads them again
  when Android takes the context away (risk R6). Before, they could come back empty after a long time in the background.

### Changed

- detekt: the baseline of the old findings goes from 82 to 73 (the reading of the time, of the world, of the maps, of the messages, of
  the profile, of a bundle of saves, of the recipes of the art and of the pack, and the input of Android, in smaller functions).

  the world with integer math, the same in the reference and in the two clients (scenario `traveller_walks`), and is not saved.

  by the reference and both clients, with farming, cooking, two market days in the rain, hunting, the feast of the second day, a
  walking traveller and a timed quest. A test of the reference checks its invariants (the gold never below zero, no quest stuck, the
  feast eaten, a save at the start of any day that resumes to the same week). `docs/design/suites.md` (`tools/make_suites_map.py`,
  checked in `ci-data`) says where each category of conformance tests named by the plan is among the real suites.

### Changed

- **Quests that collect are done at once** (spec 07, "Quests"): what the bag holds is counted at the start of every tick, so a quest
  that asks for berries, ore or a dish is done as soon as you have them, however they came (gathered, harvested, crafted, bought), not
  at the next kill or talk.

- The Kotlin of the core is tidier again (detekt baseline from 96 to 90 findings): the loops that find the creature to tame, to strike
  for the companion and the tamed friend, to hit with the burst and the swing, and the enemies of a loaded save have one way out each;
  the starting weapon and skills of the protagonist are read without loops; the rasterizer blends a pixel in a function of its own and
  reads a path in a loop that ends with the text; the pack reads the texts of a file in a function of its own. Nothing of the game changes
  (the conformance vectors check it).

- The Kotlin is a little tidier (detekt baseline from 104 to 96 findings): a failed read of a game, of the profile or of the language
  is written to the log instead of being swallowed, an invalid JSON file of the pack says why, three loops of the pack, the font and
  the texts have one way out each, and the input of the chooser of the protagonists is split in two simpler functions.

- **The checks of GitHub take a fraction of the time.** The desktop builds rebuilt SDL3 and every dependency on every run: the cache of vcpkg had a key that never changed, so a cache of 3 November whose binaries no longer matched the compilers of the runners was restored (vcpkg used none of it) and never replaced. Every run now saves the binaries it built and the next restores the newest; the tools vcpkg downloads are kept too, and the Linux build reuses the objects of unchanged files (ccache). The check of the smoke-test frames reads the PNGs with byte slices instead of pixel by pixel (from three minutes to nine seconds on this machine, with the same fingerprints).

- Every generator writes LF line endings (on Windows they wrote CRLF, and every run showed hundreds of files as changed).
