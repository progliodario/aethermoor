# Changelog

Format based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), versions follow [SemVer](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed

- Italian accents on desktop: the font of SDL draws the ASCII characters only, so every è, à, ù, ò and ì (about 430 in the texts) and the « » of the recipes were drawn as a block. They are now drawn as the letter with a small accent (and the quotation marks as ASCII ones). Found by looking at a frame of a dialogue.
- Texts with accents on desktop are aligned by characters, not by bytes: the clock, the gold, the prices, the ranks, the map names and the title were a little too far to the left; the name of the region at the top uses the same accented font.
- The desktop code is formatted with clang-format (`desktop/.clang-format`, 120 columns; 5 percent of the lines were longer than the 100 it asked for): only whitespace and the line breaks of comments changed, checked file by file. `tools/ship.py` formats the C++ files it commits from now on.
- On the world map a name that would be drawn over another one is left out (towns first, then villages, then dungeons), so the names near the start are readable; Lumeria is a little wetter, and the entrance of the Cinder Citadel has rocks instead of green bushes.
- Regions look like their names (spec 09, "Regions look like their names"): Sunscar was snow with pines, Frostveil a meadow with oaks, the Ashen Wastes a green field. Each region now bends the terrain (a blend of biases over 4500 tiles), has ground tiles of its own (sixteen new ones) and its own random props (a new dead tree for the ash).
- In the glade, the lairs and the nests of grass, creatures and lamps no longer stand on gray cobblestone squares.
- The shop, the bag and the list of Threshold Stones scroll with the cursor when they have more rows than the screen can show (desktop and Android); before, the lowest entries were out of reach.
- Dungeons, lairs, nests and outposts no longer end up under a lake or inside a mountain range for some world seeds: around every landmark (spec 09, "Clearings") water and mountains become plains for 24 tiles beyond its footprint.

### Added

- **The last two protagonists of the plan** (spec 18): **Lena Voss** (a forensic doctor: a death save every forty seconds, a scalpel, a magical autopsy strike) and **Gideon Valmor** (the Demon King, reincarnated: creatures notice him at a third of the distance and healing works at 60 percent, a black blade and a wide Dominion blow). All eight protagonists are playable, each with a story, a keepsake and two personal quests with Pip. The protagonist screen puts the preview of the character on the right so that the longer list does not touch it.
- The protagonist screen shows the character (its palette, idle animation), the primary stats and the health, mana and mana regeneration of level 1; Daichi's Eternal Aegis has its own square next to the cooldown squares (gold, lit when the death save is ready). The smoke test saves the title and protagonist screens (`title.png`, `chooser_0.png` ...) so they can be looked at.
- **Gift rules and two more protagonists** (spec 18, "Gift rules"; spec 05): a protagonist can have rules for its gift (`gift_rules`: five whole numbers, neutral by default): `mp_regen_pct`, `aggro_pct`, `heal_pct`, `dodge_cooldown_pct` and `death_save_ticks`. Sakura regenerates mana three times as fast, Daichi's Eternal Aegis leaves him on his feet with one health point once every thirty seconds, creatures notice Haruka at half the distance, and the new protagonists are **Marco Ferri** (an engineer and cook: food and potions heal twice as much, a wrench and a pressure blast) and **Yuki Shirogane** (an art thief: the dodge recovers twice as fast, a thin dagger, a very fast shadow step and a shadow cut). Both have a story, a keepsake and two personal quests with Pip like the others. Reference, both clients and five new conformance scenarios (`gift_death_save`, `gift_mp_regen`, `gift_heal`, `gift_aggro`, `gift_dodge_cooldown`).
- **A personal story for each protagonist** (spec 18, "Personal stories"): each one starts with a keepsake (an office badge, a lab notebook, a helmet strap, a clinic card) and Pip, the lad of the home village, has a question for it in his menu that opens two quests in order, written for that character (Ren's overtime and expense report, Sakura's reagents and calibration run, Daichi's wolves and roofs, Haruka's poultice and quiet pen), paying experience, gold, potions and a unique rare weapon (Ledger blade, Focus staff, Rescue axe, Herbalist cane). 8 quests, 8 items and about 60 lines of text in Italian and English; data only.
- **Four protagonists** (spec 18): a new game asks who to be, on a screen with each one's story and gift (desktop and Android; `--protagonist=<name>` on desktop). Besides **Ren** there are **Sakura Mori** (a physics student with a huge mana pool and magical blows: Spark and Arcane burst, a staff), **Daichi Okabe** (a firefighter with a lot of health and defense, an axe, a shield roll and a heavy slam) and **Haruka Aizawa** (a vet, fast and lucky, a walking stick and a gale strike). Each has base stats and growth, a weapon, three skills, starting items, a palette of the character art (three new variants of the four body parts) and a story and a gift text in Italian and English. The choice is kept in `protagonist.txt` in the folder of the slot; older games are played as Ren. No gift has a rule of its own yet: the texts say what each one is today. Only Ren has Analyze.
- Magical skills (spec 05, "Damage kind"): an attack skill with `damage.kind: magical` hits with matk (intelligence and wisdom) instead of atk. Reference, both clients and three conformance scenarios; Sakura's skills use it.
- Two small squares next to the bars show the cooldowns of the dodge roll (green, `L`) and the power strike (blue, `K`, lit only with enough mana). Both clients.
- Mana potions (spec 06, new item effect `restore_mp`): a small one (30 mana, 40 gold) and a medium one (80 mana, 110 gold), sold in Verdalis, the Whisperwood, Ironspine, Frostveil and Lumeria (the medium one in the Whisperwood and Lumeria). A scenario (`use_mana_potion`) in both clients.
- Mana and a second attack (spec 05, "Mana"; roadmap M3): the mana pool of the stats (spec 04: `max_mp` and `mp_regen_x100`, until now unused) is now kept, shown as a blue bar under the health bar, regenerates exactly (in 1/6000 points per tick), and is spent by the power strike (`K`, gamepad `RB`, the Strike button on Android; skill `base:skill/ren/power_strike`): a wide heavy blow, power 230 percent, 10 mana, cooldown of 90 ticks. A death, a Threshold Stone and a bed at an inn refill it; it is not saved (a loaded game starts full). Reference, both clients and four new conformance scenarios (`power_strike`, `power_strike_without_skill`, `strike_without_mana`, `strike_wins_over_attack`).
- More green slimes: they are now the most common creature of Verdalis (weight 6 of 10 instead of 1 of 5), commoner in the Whisperwood, Sunscar and Tidehaven (weights 5, 3 and 3) and they live in every region (weight 2 in Ironspine, Frostveil, the Ashen Wastes and Lumeria); and there are about a third more enemy spawners everywhere (`spawners.per_10000` from 8 to 11). The world vectors change (fauna, chunks, window, settlements, guild); data only, no code change.
- Ren's Analyze, the eye of the system (roadmap M3, skill `base:skill/ren/analyze`): `Z` (a button on Android) shows, for six seconds, the nearest living creature within 10 tiles with its level, health, attack, defense and what it drops with the chance, in a panel at the top center. Presentation only (no rules, no vectors); the weaknesses are not shown because elements have no effect yet. Both clients.
- The dodge roll (spec 05, "Dodge roll"; roadmap M3): `L` or `Left Shift` (gamepad `B`, the Dodge button on Android) rolls about two tiles in the direction you move (or the way you face), with 14 ticks of invulnerability, and can be used again 40 ticks later (not during a swing; with the attack input, the roll wins). It is a new skill of the base pack (`base:skill/common/dodge`, a skill of type `movement` with a `movement` block in the schema), part of Ren's starting skills. Reference, both clients and five new conformance scenarios (`dodge_roll`, `dodge_cooldown`, `dodge_iframes`, `dodge_without_skill`, `dodge_and_attack`).

### Changed

- **More detailed graphics, part 1: the terrain and the props** (style guide in `docs/design/art-style.md`): the 34 terrain tiles have soft patches of tone, tufts, flowers, pebbles, planks with grain, masonry with courses, dunes, drifts, embers and glints (they were nearly flat colors); the 17 props (trees, rocks, bushes, cacti, camps, crates, barrels, signs, statues, tents, banners, wells, Threshold Stones, lamps with a glow) have gradients from the light at the top left, highlights, shadows on the ground, bark, leaves, stones and details. They are written by `tools/make_art.py` (`artkit.py`, `art_props.py`); the sizes and the origins are the same, so nothing else changes.
- **More detailed graphics, part 2: people, creatures and the interface**: the head of every person has ears, cheeks, eyes with a white, an iris and a glint, eyebrows, a mouth and a fringe with strands; the torso has a collar, folds, a belt with a buckle and a lit shoulder line; the arms have cuffs and hands with fingers; the legs have creases and boots with a sole and a toe cap (`tools/art_chars.py`, every palette and protagonist variant kept). The 60 creatures gain details on top of their recipes (`tools/art_creatures.py`): jelly with a thick base, bubbles, a mouth and eye glints for the slimes and the matrons and sovereigns; fur strokes, a ruff, brows, whiskers and glints for the wolves; plates, rivets, glowing cracks, rounded shoulders and a lit visor for the knights; a highlight, a shade and eye glints for the bat, spider, scorpion, crab, owl, myconid, golem and wisp. The panels of the interface (bag, journal, shop, dialogue, notices) have a shadow, a double border, a body that fades from light to dark, a light line, a stripe and studs in the corners, and the health, mana and experience bars have a frame, a lit top half, a gloss line and ticks (both clients).
- **Graphics, part 3: every protagonist and every creature looks different** (spec 13): a recipe layer can name the variants it belongs to (`only`) or does not belong to (`except`), so a variant has its own shapes and not only its own colors (both clients, with two new raster vectors). Ren wears glasses and a tie; Sakura has twin tails, goggles and a lab coat; Daichi a fire helmet, reflective bands and boots; Haruka a ponytail and a clinic apron with a paw; Marco a bandana, a mustache and a cook's apron; Yuki a beret, a domino mask and a red scarf; Lena a bun, glasses and a long coat; Gideon horns, pointed ears, fangs, a circlet and a cape. The villagers have a hat or hairdo each (cap, hood, straw hat, mage hat, headband, baker's hat, flat cap and beard). Every slime has its own feature (sprout, drop, flames, crystals, cracks of lava, smoke, lantern, moss and mushroom, mint leaves, coral, pearl, stars, rust and cog, dunes, stone plates, fins, trapped insect), the crowned and the giant slimes share the one of their kind, the wolves have scars, scarves, ice, stripes, lightning and crowns, and each knight a mark of its own (skull, tricorn and anchor, hammer, hard hat and lamp, ice spikes, turban and spear, keys and lock). `tools/art_looks.py` and `tools/art_creature_looks.py` hold them; `art_preview.py --figures` draws every person.
- **Graphics, part 4: the houses**: the houses of the hamlets, villages, towns and outposts are timber and plaster with posts, beams, a brace and windows with a pot of flowers, and have a real door with a stone threshold (three new tiles, `wall_timber`, `wall_window` and `door`, gids 35 to 37 of the overworld tileset); keeps, dungeons and ruins stay stone. `tools/make_stamps.py` builds them (`:`, `'` and `;`), and the world vectors are regenerated.
- **Titles make you stronger** (spec 11; both clients, the new combat vector `title_attack`): every title you have earned adds to the attack of your blows, 2 to 10 percent each, up to 65 percent with all fifteen. The journal shows the bonus of each title and the total.
- **The journal says more**: after the quests it now shows your companion (its name, whether it waits or follows, its bond and the attack the bond adds), your way of playing (gatherings, things made, blows parried) and the echoes of past lives, before titles and bestiary. Both clients.
- **Fights are easy on purpose**: the creatures of the world now have their plain health (it was doubled) and 70 percent of their attack (it was 175 percent). The hero of an isekai is overpowered: an ordinary creature falls in a blow or two and barely hurts.
- **The map shows more land**: walking reveals a square of 5 x 5 map cells around you (640 tiles across) instead of 3 x 3. A game already started gains the wider view as you walk.
- **New READMEs**: the two main pages of the project (Italian and English) were rewritten from scratch: what is in the game, the protagonists, how to try it, the controls, how the project is built, the folders, how to develop, what is missing.
- **Housekeeping**: the tables of the specs and of the milestones say what is there today, the guide to adding content covers recipes, gathering, companions, titles, prologues and sounds, and the eight branches of merged work were deleted.
- **Fixed: the disturbing sound that never stopped.** The loops of ambience of the regions (wind, leaves, birds, crickets, magic) are switched off: they were made of rough stepped noise and, in Verdalis by day, of a whistle that climbed for eight seconds and started again. The rain is less than half as loud and the footsteps half as loud. The recipes stay in the pack for when the synthesizer can make softer noise.
- **Fixed: the `E` hint always said "Talk".** It now says what `E` will do: talk to a person, touch a Threshold Stone, or gather from a bush, a tree, a rock or a cactus (a new hint over the prop on the desktop; the word on the Android button).
- **Prologues** (spec 18): a new game now opens with a short talk with Lyra at the Threshold, different for each of the eight protagonists and in their tone (Ren and the truck, Sakura and her machine, Daichi and the third floor, Haruka and the dog, Marco and the pressure cooker, Yuki and the jump, Lena and her notes, Gideon and his lost afternoon), with one choice each. Both clients, Italian and English.
- **Cooking and more recipes** (spec 06): eight new recipes, sixteen in all. Cooked dishes (hearty stew from spore caps, fish stew from crab shells, a royal feast from a stew and a honey cake), a large health potion and a medium mana potion from the smaller ones, and gear from hides and ore (a leather vest, a studded one, a pike). The crafting panel now scrolls; on Android a tap chooses a recipe and a second tap makes it.
- **Companion commands and bond** (spec 10; both clients, two new combat vectors `companion_stays` and `companion_bond`): `H` on the desktop and a Stay / Follow button on Android tell your companion to wait where it is (it still fights what comes near, and comes back by itself if you go far). Every creature it kills grows its bond: one more point of attack every five kills, up to ten. Both are saved.
- **Echoes** (spec 11; both clients, the new combat vector `echo_health`): every title you have ever earned in any game now adds one percent (up to fifteen) to the maximum health of all your new games and of the ones you load. It is the first form of reincarnation.
- **Shared profile** (spec 12, "Profile"; new conformance suite `profile`, both clients): a `profile.json` next to the settings keeps the best count of every creature killed and every title earned in any game, and never goes down. The title screen shows it (kinds of creatures and titles). It is the base of the reincarnation.
- **Guardians break in notches**: the 27 guardians (the big creatures of the sites) have a stagger bar of 25 percent of their health instead of 40, so a long fight breaks them several times.
- **Export and import on Android** (spec 12): the title screen of the Android client now has an Export button on every used game and an Import button on every empty one, as the desktop has keys. The files are in the `exports` folder of the app's storage. Never tried on a device.
- **Skills have elements** (spec 05; both clients, the new combat vector `skill_element`): a skill with an element hits with it whatever the weapon. Six second skills now have one: Dominion and Shadow Cut (dark), Gale Strike (wind), Guard Slam (earth), Pressure Blast (fire) and Arcane Burst (light), so they can weaken, stun or burn and are stronger or weaker by creature.
- **Stagger** (spec 05; both clients, two new combat vectors `stagger_breaks` and `stagger_strike`; the other combat vectors and the save vectors were regenerated): your hits fill a hidden bar on every creature; when it is full (hits worth 40 percent of its health by default, `stagger_pct` in the data of a creature changes it) the creature is broken for two seconds: it cannot act and takes a quarter more damage. The power strike fills the bar twice as fast. The bar is drawn under the health of the creature: yellow while it fills, white while the creature is broken.
- **Status effects on the player** (spec 05; both clients, five new combat vectors `player_status_*`): a creature of an element can now leave its mark when it hits you: fire burns (a little health every half second, never the last point), water chills (you walk at half speed), wind and earth stun (no attack, roll or parry for a moment, but you can walk), light and dark weaken your hits. A parry stops them with the hit. They end when you die. The interface does not show them yet.
- **Play-style counters and three more titles** (spec 11; both clients, the new combat vector `counter_titles`, and `board_quest_rewards` now checks the quest title): the game counts how many times you gather, craft and parry, and saves it. Three titles ask for them: Forager (30 gatherings), Artisan (15 recipes) and Unbreakable Guard (25 parries). This is the first step of the tags.
- **Bestiary and titles** (new spec 11; both clients, the combat vector `bestiary_titles`): the game now counts every creature you defeat, by kind, and saves it. Twelve titles (Newcomer, Slime Hunter, Pest Control, Wolf Bane, Adventurer, Helper of the Folk, Naturalist, Veteran, Gel King's Bane, Hero, Chronicler of Beasts, Legend of the Realm) are earned from kills, level, kinds met and quests done. The journal shows both after the quests: the titles (the ones not earned yet as `???`) and the bestiary with a count for each creature.
- **Companions** (new spec 10; both clients, two new combat vectors `companion_fights` and `companion_follows`): every protagonist now has a small companion that walks beside them and fights: a wisp called Orb for Ren, an owl called Newton for Sakura, a wolf called Ember for Daichi, a mint slime called Pip for Haruka, a crab called Pot for Marco, a bat called Shade for Yuki, a spider called Mort for Lena and a shadow wolf called Gloom for Gideon. It hits the nearest creature in reach every second or so (its attack grows with your level), cannot be hurt, and what it kills counts as yours. It is drawn with the art of its creature, a little smaller. Commands, bonds and several companions at once come later.
- **Crafting** (spec 06, "Crafting"; both clients, new combat vector `craft_recipes`): with the materials you gather you can make things from the bag, anywhere: tonics (two for one herb and two berries), small and medium health potions (herbs, slime gel, pine resin), a small mana potion (mint leaves and a herb), a travel ration, honey cakes, a hand axe and an iron sword. `C` opens the panel on the desktop (a button on Android); recipes you can make are green, each shows how much of every ingredient you have. A sound for it.
- **Gathering** (spec 06, "Gathering"; both clients, new combat vector `gather_nodes`): the bushes, trees, rocks and cacti of the world can be gathered from with `E` (on Android the talk button shows up near them): berries and healing herbs from the bushes, logs, resin and dry branches from the trees, stone and a little iron ore from the rocks, pulp from the cacti. Eight new materials, some of which heal when eaten. What you find depends on the world and the prop, not on chance you can re-roll; a prop grows back after a few minutes. The materials are for the crafting that comes next.
- **Export and import of a game** (spec 12, "Export and import"; new conformance suite `savebundle`, both clients; the buttons on the desktop only): a game can be packed into one `.aethersave` file and unpacked into an empty slot, to back it up or move it to another device. On the title screen `X` writes the chosen game to `exports/slot<N>.aethersave` next to the saves, and `I` reads `exports/import.aethersave` into the chosen empty game. A file that is not a valid bundle is refused without writing anything. Android has the format but no buttons yet.
- **Roadmap updated** (no change to the game): `docs/plan/06-roadmap-rischi-backlog.md` marks the sounds and the music (switched off), the elements, the status effects, the parry and the volume settings as done, with what is left of each.
- **The parry** (spec 05, "Parry"; both clients, two new combat vectors): `U` (the left shoulder button of a gamepad, a button on Android) opens a window of a quarter of a second in which an attack that lands does no damage, stuns the creature for a second and leaves you safe for a moment; a second parry can be tried after a second and a bit. During the window you cannot attack or roll, so it must be timed. There is a new clang of a sound for it.
- **Status effects** (spec 05, "Status effects"; both clients, five new combat vectors `status_*`): a hit with an elemental weapon may leave an effect on the creature: fire burns it (damage every half second for two and a half), water chills it (half speed), wind and earth stun it (it does nothing for a moment), light and dark weaken it (it hits at three quarters). The chance depends on the element (30 to 40 percent), creatures immune to the element are spared. Together with the elements this makes the choice of weapon matter. The parry is next.
- **Volume settings** (spec 16, "Settings"; both clients): four volumes (all of it, the effects, the ambience, the music) kept in `settings.json`. On the desktop `F10` opens a panel with a bar for each, changed with `A` and `D`; on Android a sound button next to the language button cycles the master volume (100, 60, 30, 0 percent). Without the file the effects are at 80 percent and the ambience and the music at 50.
- **The ambience is lower and softer**: the birds, the crickets, the leaves and the rain were pure high tones and hiss (3 to 5 kHz) that played all the time and were piercing. They are now a third lower in pitch (triangle waves, 1.3 to 2.6 kHz, noise at 0.9 to 2.5 kHz) and much quieter, and each region mixes them at a lower level.
- **The music is switched off for now** (the owner asked): the pack no longer has `music_rules.json`, so neither client plays any music, the title screen included. The 33 pieces, the renderer and the music director stay; `python tools/compose.py --rules` writes the rules again and brings the music back. Sounds and ambience are not touched.
- **Music that follows you** (spec 14, both clients, new conformance suites `music` and `music_director`): 33 pieces composed in the repository (`tools/compose.py`: a mode, a key, a tempo, a chord progression and instruments for each mood; a pad, a bass, an arpeggio and a melody on every chord) and played as seamless loops: a day and a night theme for each of the eight regions (Verdalis bright, Whisperwood mysterious, Ironspine heavy, Sunscar in a desert minor, Frostveil cold and slow, Tidehaven sailing, the Ashen Wastes dark, Lumeria shimmering), music for the towns and for the dungeons, a title theme, a piece for the end, a theme for the personal quests of each of the eight protagonists, and four pieces for hunts, mysteries, sorrows and festivals that follow the quest you have under way. Each piece has layers that come in as the game does: a trembling drone when you are badly hurt, drums and a driving bass in a fight, a heavy pulse, a low horn and a choir against a boss. A new piece is rendered in the background and takes over with a crossfade. `tools/audio_wav.py` writes any sound or piece to a WAV file to listen to it. The music has not been heard by its author (no sound device where it was made): please listen and say what to change.
- **A world full of sounds** (spec 14, both clients, new conformance suites `audio_director` and `audio_ambience`): the game now has 156 synthesized sounds and 109 events, all chosen by data (`data/base/audio/events.json`, schema `audio_events`): footsteps that change with the ground (grass, dirt, sand, snow, stone, wood, water, moss, ash, three variations each), four voices for each of 14 families of creatures (it notices you, it attacks, it is hit, it dies; slimes, matrons, kings, wolves, alphas, knights, bats, spiders, scorpions, crabs, owls, myconids, golems, wisps), a swing in three variants, hurt, a heartbeat when badly wounded, the death save, a thump for critical hits and a ding for weaknesses, items (pickup, coins, buying, selling, equipping a weapon or armor, drinking a potion, mana, food), the interface (a sound for opening and closing the bag, the map, the journal, the shop, the board and the travel list, moving through a menu), the dialogue (opening, each line, closing), quests (accepted, done), waking a Threshold Stone, entering a region, the first rain and its end, thunder in a storm, dawn and nightfall. Under all of it, **ambience loops** that change with the place and the hour: birds and leaves in Verdalis, crickets at night, the sea at Tidehaven, snow wind in Frostveil, desert wind in Sunscar, ash and embers in the Ashen Wastes, a magic hum in Lumeria, rain that grows with the weather and silences the birds. Creatures far away are quieter. New synth features: amplitude modulation and looping recipes. Music is next.
- **Elements in combat** (spec 05, "Elements"; both clients, new combat vector `element_hits`): a weapon can have an element (22 of them do: the fire blades, the rime and pearl weapons for water, the thunder ones for wind, the hammers for earth, the sun and nebula ones for light, the moon and black blades for dark) and a creature can have its own (29 do). A hit does the creature's `weaknesses_pm` for the weapon's element (1500 is a weakness, 0 an immunity), half when it is the creature's own element, and the normal damage otherwise; a hit on a weakness shows its number with an exclamation mark. Choose the weapon for the place: fire against the ice, water against the embers. Skills still have no element, and parry and status effects are not done yet.
- **Sound effects** (spec 14, both clients): sounds are synthesized from small recipes (`data/base/audio/sfx/`, schema `sfx_recipe`; waves, glides in pitch, noise, envelopes) with the same algorithm in C++ and Kotlin, checked by the new conformance suite `audio`. The game plays a swing, a power strike, hits and critical hits, being hurt, the dodge roll, a level up, picking things up, coins, opening and closing panels and a death. `F8` mutes on the desktop. Music, ambience and volume settings come next. The Android client plays through the audio device of libGDX and has not run on a device.
- **Graphics, part 5: the single creatures and the market**: the bat, spider, scorpion, crab, owl, myconid, stone golem and wisp each have their own details instead of a generic shine (`tools/art_single_looks.py`: wing veins, bristles, tail rings, claw teeth, feather chevrons, gills, glowing runes, inner flame); two new props, the market stall with its striped awning and the hay bale, stand on the plazas of the big towns and in the yard of the barn hamlet.
- **A general update of the repository** (no change to the game): `tools/README.md` lists every script; the guide has a section on adding a protagonist; spec 12 lists the files of a slot (`protagonist.txt`, `goal.txt`); spec 07 mentions the personal stories; the READMEs, the repo map of `CLAUDE.md`, the plan and the glossary say what exists now; the roadmap (`docs/plan/06`, section 8) has a new status table and an ordered plan of the next steps (audio first); four texts that nothing used were removed; stale comments about "M0" in the feature lists and a test name were fixed.
- **Balance pass** (the owner left the numbers to Claude): the creatures of the streamed world now have 200 percent health and 175 percent attack (`difficulty` of the world data, spec 04). Until now every fight was too easy (a first-level hero beat creatures of level 8 to 12 without effort). With the new numbers the model of `docs/design/balance.md`, which now applies the difficulty, finds no outlier: each creature is comfortable at about its own level; fights of the same level last about 2.5 seconds and cost about a fifth of the health, bosses up to 8 seconds. Data only; the vectors of the world change.
- The goal marked on the map is kept with the game (a file `goal.txt` in the folder of the slot): it is still there after quitting or switching games. Both clients.
- A small map in the top left corner shows the land around you (640 tiles across, only where you have been), with you, the Threshold Stones you woke and the goal; `N` hides it, a tap on it opens the world map on Android (spec 09, "The small map"). Both clients.
- More help to find the way (spec 09, "The map view"): a green arrow around the player points toward the goal when it is far; entering a region says its name; on the map `Tab` (the Next button on Android) jumps to the next place found, nearest first, and `P` then makes it the goal; the HUD text has a dark edge so that it reads on grass and snow. Both clients.
- Never get lost again (spec 09, "The map view"): the HUD shows your position in tiles, you can mark a goal on the map (`P`, a right click, or the Goal button on Android) and the HUD then shows its direction and distance (`Goal: north-east, 6300 tiles`), and the map shows the goal with a line from you and the Threshold Stones you woke.
