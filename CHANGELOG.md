# Changelog

Format based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), versions follow [SemVer](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed

- Italian accents on desktop: the font of SDL draws the ASCII characters only, so every è, à, ù, ò and ì (about 430 in the texts) and the « » of the recipes were drawn as a block. They are now drawn as the letter with a small accent (and the quotation marks as ASCII ones). Found by looking at a frame of a dialogue.
- Texts with accents on desktop are aligned by characters, not by bytes: the clock, the gold, the prices, the ranks, the map names and the title were a little too far to the left; the name of the region at the top uses the same accented font.
- The desktop code is formatted with clang-format (`desktop/.clang-format`, 120 columns; 5 percent of the lines were longer than the 100 it asked for): only whitespace and the line breaks of comments changed, checked file by file. `tools/ship.py` formats the C++ files it commits from now on.
- On the world map a name that would be drawn over another one is left out (towns first, then villages, then dungeons), so the names near the start are readable; Lumeria is a little wetter, and the entrance of the Cinder Citadel has rocks instead of green bushes.
- Regions look like their names (spec 09, "Regions look like their names"): Sunscar was snow with pines, Frostveil a meadow with oaks, the Ashen Wastes a green field. Each region now bends the terrain (a blend of biases over 4500 tiles), has ground tiles of its own (sixteen new ones) and its own random props (a new dead tree for the ash).
- In the glade, the lairs and the nests of grass, creatures and lamps no longer stand on gray cobblestone squares.
- The shop, the bag and the list of Threshold Stones scroll with the cursor when they have more rows than the screen can show (desktop and Android); before, the lowest entries were out of reach.
- Dungeons, lairs, nests and outposts no longer end up under a lake or inside a mountain range for some world seeds: around every landmark (spec 09, "Clearings") water and mountains become plains for 24 tiles beyond its footprint.

### Added

- **Four protagonists** (spec 18): a new game asks who to be, on a screen with each one's story and gift (desktop and Android; `--protagonist=<name>` on desktop). Besides **Ren** there are **Sakura Mori** (a physics student with a huge mana pool and magical blows: Spark and Arcane burst, a staff), **Daichi Okabe** (a firefighter with a lot of health and defense, an axe, a shield roll and a heavy slam) and **Haruka Aizawa** (a vet, fast and lucky, a walking stick and a gale strike). Each has base stats and growth, a weapon, three skills, starting items, a palette of the character art (three new variants of the four body parts) and a story and a gift text in Italian and English. The choice is kept in `protagonist.txt` in the folder of the slot; older games are played as Ren. No gift has a rule of its own yet: the texts say what each one is today. Only Ren has Analyze.
- Magical skills (spec 05, "Damage kind"): an attack skill with `damage.kind: magical` hits with matk (intelligence and wisdom) instead of atk. Reference, both clients and three conformance scenarios; Sakura's skills use it.
- Two small squares next to the bars show the cooldowns of the dodge roll (green, `L`) and the power strike (blue, `K`, lit only with enough mana). Both clients.
- Mana potions (spec 06, new item effect `restore_mp`): a small one (30 mana, 40 gold) and a medium one (80 mana, 110 gold), sold in Verdalis, the Whisperwood, Ironspine, Frostveil and Lumeria (the medium one in the Whisperwood and Lumeria). A scenario (`use_mana_potion`) in both clients.
- Mana and a second attack (spec 05, "Mana"; roadmap M3): the mana pool of the stats (spec 04: `max_mp` and `mp_regen_x100`, until now unused) is now kept, shown as a blue bar under the health bar, regenerates exactly (in 1/6000 points per tick), and is spent by the power strike (`K`, gamepad `RB`, the Strike button on Android; skill `base:skill/ren/power_strike`): a wide heavy blow, power 230 percent, 10 mana, cooldown of 90 ticks. A death, a Threshold Stone and a bed at an inn refill it; it is not saved (a loaded game starts full). Reference, both clients and four new conformance scenarios (`power_strike`, `power_strike_without_skill`, `strike_without_mana`, `strike_wins_over_attack`).
- More green slimes: they are now the most common creature of Verdalis (weight 6 of 10 instead of 1 of 5), commoner in the Whisperwood, Sunscar and Tidehaven (weights 5, 3 and 3) and they live in every region (weight 2 in Ironspine, Frostveil, the Ashen Wastes and Lumeria); and there are about a third more enemy spawners everywhere (`spawners.per_10000` from 8 to 11). The world vectors change (fauna, chunks, window, settlements, guild); data only, no code change.
- Ren's Analyze, the eye of the system (roadmap M3, skill `base:skill/ren/analyze`): `Z` (a button on Android) shows, for six seconds, the nearest living creature within 10 tiles with its level, health, attack, defense and what it drops with the chance, in a panel at the top center. Presentation only (no rules, no vectors); the weaknesses are not shown because elements have no effect yet. Both clients.
- The dodge roll (spec 05, "Dodge roll"; roadmap M3): `L` or `Left Shift` (gamepad `B`, the Dodge button on Android) rolls about two tiles in the direction you move (or the way you face), with 14 ticks of invulnerability, and can be used again 40 ticks later (not during a swing; with the attack input, the roll wins). It is a new skill of the base pack (`base:skill/common/dodge`, a skill of type `movement` with a `movement` block in the schema), part of Ren's starting skills. Reference, both clients and five new conformance scenarios (`dodge_roll`, `dodge_cooldown`, `dodge_iframes`, `dodge_without_skill`, `dodge_and_attack`).

### Changed

- The goal marked on the map is kept with the game (a file `goal.txt` in the folder of the slot): it is still there after quitting or switching games. Both clients.
- A small map in the top left corner shows the land around you (640 tiles across, only where you have been), with you, the Threshold Stones you woke and the goal; `N` hides it, a tap on it opens the world map on Android (spec 09, "The small map"). Both clients.
- More help to find the way (spec 09, "The map view"): a green arrow around the player points toward the goal when it is far; entering a region says its name; on the map `Tab` (the Next button on Android) jumps to the next place found, nearest first, and `P` then makes it the goal; the HUD text has a dark edge so that it reads on grass and snow. Both clients.
- Never get lost again (spec 09, "The map view"): the HUD shows your position in tiles, you can mark a goal on the map (`P`, a right click, or the Goal button on Android) and the HUD then shows its direction and distance (`Goal: north-east, 6300 tiles`), and the map shows the goal with a line from you and the Threshold Stones you woke. Both clients.
- `docs/design/catalog.md` lists the world position (x, y, as the map shows it) of every site, next to its offset from the start.
- The world map shows the land in detail: a cell of 128 x 128 tiles used to be one color (the biome of its center), so coasts and ranges were lost; zoomed in, a cell is now a grid of up to 16 x 16 squares (8 x 8 tiles each), each with the biome of its own tile (spec 09, "The map view", `biome_code_at`, new vectors of the `worldmap` suite checked by both clients).
- The world map is much easier to read and to get around in (spec 09, "The map view", spec 16): it opens framed on everything you have explored (at the start the 3 x 3 cells around you fill the view, instead of a few pixels in a 128 x 128 grid), with five zoom levels (the whole world to sixteen times closer), a view you move with `WASD` or the arrows, by dragging, with the wheel or the pinch, `C` to go back to yourself and `0` to frame it all again (on Android: drag, pinch, and buttons `+`, `-`, `You`, `All`, `X`). Dungeons, lairs and nests now have a violet marker next to the yellow settlements, hamlets get names when you zoom in, your position in tiles and a legend are shown under the map, and you are still shown when you are outside the view. Names go to the first free side of their marker (right, left, below, above) and never over another marker or name, and map text has a dark edge so that it reads on pale cells. Both clients; Android compiled and tested by CI only. Pip tells new players about it.
- All the Markdown files were checked against the code and brought up to date (the details are in the pull request): `README` (how to try the build, honest status), `desktop/README` (flags, controls, layout, tests), `android/README`, spec 00 (the day lasts 20 minutes), 02, 03 and 16 (no longer "M2 subsets": what the engine does, the real order of a tick, every key, button and gamepad binding), `docs/dev/setup.md` (what CI runs and what it does not), the glossary, the modding notes, `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` (the real dependencies; ThorVG, SDL3_ttf and miniz are not used), notes on the ADRs that are only partly built (0004, 0007, 0008, 0009, 0011) and a status table at the top of every plan document.
- `tools/ship.py` (checks, signed-off commit, push, pull request, wait for CI, squash merge, refresh the play build; formats the changed C++ with clang-format; reuses only an open pull request for a branch) and a faster `tools/gen_conformance.py` (the suites are built in parallel, about 20 s, and only changed files are written).
- CI: the vcpkg binaries are cached (the desktop build is much faster), the playable Windows and Linux builds are uploaded on every run with `desktop/PLAY.txt`, and Dependabot no longer proposes Kotlin, libGDX and Android Gradle plugin upgrades, which need code changes (they are done by hand).
- Desktop developer flags: `--start=X,Y` (put the player at a tile), `--slot=N` (open a game without the title screen), `--map=demo|world`, `--seed=N`, `--screenshot-dir=DIR`.
- `docs/guides/add-content.md`: how to add an item, a creature, a quest and its dialogue, a place, a region and a language, with the checks to run.

### Added

- A difficulty knob in the world data (spec 04, "Difficulty"): `difficulty {enemy_hp_pct, enemy_atk_pct}` scales the health and the attack of every creature of the streamed world, 100 percent by default. It is the one number to change to make fights longer or harder (see `docs/design/balance.md`).
- `tools/balance_report.py` and `docs/design/balance.md`: a rough model of every fight (Ren with the gear of a typical shop at each level against each creature, from the formulas of spec 04), to find outliers when tuning. Its finding: by the formulas every creature is comfortable far below its level and the bosses of the dungeons die in 2 to 4 seconds, so combat is probably too easy; the numbers are the owner's to tune.
- Four more meadow tiles (daisies, clover and pebbles, dry grass, a mushroom in a dark patch), so the plains do not repeat every two tiles. The plains pick among six tiles now (spec 09).
- Pip, a lad of the home village who answers six questions of a new player (how to fight, heal and earn money, what the stones are, where to go first, how not to get lost), in Italian and English.
- Mira closes the ring of the wayfarers: when all eight stories are finished she pays 2500 XP, 6000 gold and the epic Ring-walker's mantle.
- `docs/design/catalog.md`, generated by `tools/make_catalog.py`: the 8 regions, 60 creatures, 110 items, 94 quests and 35 sites of the pack in tables, to review the content at a glance.
- A seasonal job from every elder, offered only in its season (spec 07): the harvest cart in autumn, the wisp bloom and the festival of petals in spring, winter stores and the cold ash in winter, the short summer and the solstice survey in summer, the storm season in autumn. Out of season the elder says when to come back.
- Content consistency tests: everything the quests and dialogues name exists, every kill target spawns somewhere and every item asked for can be found.
- Two new dialogue conditions (spec 07): `season` and `tod_between` (a range of the time of day in permille, crossing midnight when it starts after it ends). Villagers now talk about the season (four lines per tone, in Italian and English) and innkeepers tell a tale about the night, only at night.
- Bushes in the plains and the forest, rocks and bones in the desert and the snow: the wild looks fuller.
- Eight creatures with new silhouettes (cave bat, cave spider, myconid, stone golem, will-o'-the-wisp, shore crab, dune scorpion, night owl: levels 4 to 12) with their materials, in the fauna of the regions; the slime elites of the lairs and nests wear a crown.
- Eight new props (tent, signpost, crate, statue, banner, bush, bones, campfire) placed in the outposts, lairs and dungeon entrances.
- `tools/art_preview.py`: draws the art recipes as an HTML sheet to look at them without the game.
- Eight nests, one per region (Mint Hollow close to the start, then Mossy Den, Rust Pit, Dune Den, Polar Rookery, Pearl Shoal, Nebula Pool and Char Pit): rings of the newer creatures with an elite in the middle, level 5 to 16, each dropping a unique rare or epic piece of equipment.
- Wayfarers (spec 07): eight named people, one in each outpost, each with a story in three quests (a task, a letter carried to another wayfarer across the map and back, and a big hunt) and a unique rare or epic reward. The letters form a ring through the eight regions. Italian and English.
- Enemy behaviors: five new sets of numbers for the melee chaser (skittish, stalker, brute, lunger, guardian), given to 35 creatures: bosses and elites stay near their posts, wolves hunt from afar or lunge, slate and rust slimes hit hard and slowly, mint and sand slimes are shy.
- Dialogue engine (spec 07): new condition `gold_at_least` and new effects `spend_gold` and `heal_full` (schemas, reference, both clients, two conformance scenarios), used by the inns.
- Inns: every innkeeper rents a bed (full health for 8 to 18 gold) and sells food to take along.
- Guild commissions: each guild clerk gives three two-stage commissions per region (hunt, then gather), unlocked by level and in order, paid in XP, gold, guild points and potions.
- Eight outposts (small camps with a shop, an inn, a well and a Threshold Stone) near the dungeons of each region and in Verdalis, and four deep dungeons for the end of Mira's chain (Deep Forge, Sunken Galleon, Glacier Keep, Obsidian Vault), whose bosses drop epic equipment of attack 35 to 41 and defense 14.
- 18 more items (a full ladder of weapons, armors and foods, up to the dragon-scale vest and the royal feast), sold in the shops of the regions; nine more creatures with materials, in the fauna of every region.
- Gossip from villagers (three lines each, per tone) and three more looks for hamlets, villages and towns.
- A second errand from every elder: once the first one is paid, they ask you to hunt the strongest creature of their region (the Gel King, the Moonshade Alpha, the lair elites), and pay in XP, gold, guild points and large potions.
- Workshops (spec 07, "Workshops"): the shopkeeper of each region makes one unique piece of equipment from monster parts (eight pieces, up to attack 24 or magic defense 14), using a new dialogue effect `remove_item` (spec 07, schemas, reference, both clients, a new conformance scenario). Elites guard the middle of every creature lair and drop unique weapons and armor. Villagers and shopkeepers talk about the state of the road. Hamlets, villages and towns have more looks (two more hamlets, two more villages, one open town). Italian and English.
- More world to explore and more to do in it: six new creatures with their art and materials (dire wolves, magma slimes, amber slimes, storm wolves, coral slimes, ice slimes, levels 7 to 10, in the regions that match), six creature lairs on the map (Dire Den, Magma Vent, Coral Grotto, Amber Hollow, Ice Pit, Storm Roost), ten new items (medium and large potions, three foods, two swords up to attack 20, three armors) sold in the shops of the regions, two bigger guild jobs (`purge` and `stockpile`), a favor from the innkeeper of every region, and a legend from villagers and innkeepers about the dungeon of their region. Italian and English.
- Back to the title screen (spec 12, "Slots"): `Esc` in a game (desktop; the Back key on Android) saves it and returns to the list of three games, so you can switch to another world without restarting. `Esc` on the list, or closing the window, quits. Panels still close first.
- A local errand from the elders (spec 07, "Local errands"): each region's village elder now has a job of their own, a side quest that asks to hunt the region's creatures and bring back what they drop (slime gel in Verdalis, shade wisps in the Whisperwood, slate shards in Ironspine, sand slimes' gel in Sunscar, frost pelts in Frostveil, tide pearls in Tidehaven, ember cores in the Ashen Wastes, star dust in Lumeria). Ask the elder for work, come back when it is done, and they pay in XP, gold and potions. It shows in the journal like any quest. Data and texts only, in Italian and English.
- A dungeon for each of the four regions that had none: the Whisperwood Hollow (a glade inside a ring of trees, far north-west; shade wolves and the Moonshade Alpha, who drops the Shadehide cloak), the Sunken Ziggurat in Sunscar (three rings of walls with gates on opposite sides, far south-east; sand slimes and the Sand Sentinel, who drops the Sunbrand), the Starfall Observatory in Lumeria (a domed hall with two libraries, far south; glow slimes and the Astral Curator, who drops the Starweave robe) and the Cinder Citadel in the Ashen Wastes (a burned fortress with four bastions, far east; ember slimes and the Cinder Colossus, who drops the Cinderblade, attack 37). Each has a Threshold Stone at its entrance and a guild quest from Mira, whose chain now runs nine quests long.
- Two more dungeons, both far expeditions: the Drowned Temple in Tidehaven (far south-west; tide slimes and the Tide Regent, who drops the Tidescale mail) and the Frostveil Cavern (far north; frost wolves and the Rime Revenant, who drops the Rimeblade, attack 30). Mira's chain goes on after the mine with "The Drowned Temple" and "The Frostveil Cavern". New bosses with recolored art, items, quests and texts (it + en); data and the stamp tool only.
- Ironspine Mine, the first dungeon outside Verdalis, far to the north-east of the start (a long expedition; wake the Threshold Stones of the towns on the way): three galleries of slate slimes, grey wolves and shade wolves, and the Ironbound Foreman in the north hall, who drops the Foreman's maul (attack 26). Mira's chain goes on with "The Ironspine Mine" after the cellar (260 XP, 600 gold, 150 guild points). New enemy, art, weapon, quest and texts (it + en); the stamp tool gets the mine; no client code changes.
- Regional fauna for the last four regions: slate slimes in Ironspine (tough, drop slate shards), shade wolves in Whisperwood (fast, drop shade wisps), tide slimes in Tidehaven (drop tide pearls) and glow slimes in Lumeria (level 7, drop star dust). New recolored art, drops and texts (it + en); the guild board asks for them in the towns of those regions. Data only; the world vectors change.
- Save slots (spec 12, "Slots"): the game opens on a title screen with three games, each with its own world, progress and saves. A used game shows its level and gold; pick one with W/S and E, the number keys or the mouse (desktop), or a tap (Android), and a new game starts a new world. A game is deleted with D twice (desktop) or the cross twice (Android). The single save of older builds becomes game 1. Desktop `--slot=N` skips the title screen. Changing games means restarting the game.
- More regional fauna: sand slimes in Sunscar, frost wolves in Frostveil and ember slimes in the Ashen Wastes (new art, levels 4, 6 and 8, new drops: frost pelt and ember core). The guild board asks for these in the towns of those regions. Data only; the world vectors change.
- Mossveil Cellar, the second dungeon of Verdalis, north-east of the start: green and blue slimes in a flooded cellar and the Gel King (a crowned giant slime) in its north room, who drops the Gel mail. Mira's chain goes on with "The Mossveil Cellar" after the barrow. New enemy, art, armor, quest and texts (it + en); no client code changes, the landmark system is data.
- Grunmoor Barrow, the first dungeon (spec 09, "Landmarks and dungeons"), north-west of the start in Verdalis: a walled barrow with a corridor, two side rooms of wolves and blue slimes, a north hall and the Barrow Warden (boss, drops the Warden's blade), with a Threshold Stone at the door. Mira offers the quest "The Barrow Warden" once the slime job is done. New: `landmarks` in the world data, `spawner` in the stamp legend, enemy, art, item and texts.
- Seasons (spec 08): the year has four seasons of eight days. Summers have longer days, winters shorter ones, the weather odds change with the season (more rain in spring, clear skies in summer), the screen takes a seasonal tint and the clock shows the season. Conformance suite `worldtime` covers the season edges.
- NPC routines (spec 08, 09): settlement people keep hours. Shopkeepers and guild clerks open in the morning and go home at dusk (a closed shop has nobody to talk to), villagers and elders gather in the plaza in the evening and go indoors at night, innkeepers never leave. Stamps got gathering `spots`; conformance suite `routines`; people carry their plaza position in `worldgen_chunks`.
- Threshold Stones (spec 09, 12): a glowing stone stands in the plaza of every hamlet, village and town. Press `E` next to it (or tap the interact button) to wake it, be fully healed and see the list of stones you woke; pick one to travel there at once. Woken stones are saved. New stamp character `T`, the stone art, world data `stones`; the world vectors change for every client.
- Regional fauna (spec 09): every region has its own enemies. New blue slimes (tougher, from level 3) and grey wolves (fast, level 4, drop wolf fangs) with their art, texts and item; the enemy of a spawner is a weighted pick among the fauna of its region, so the home meadow keeps its green slimes while the rest of the world is more dangerous. Guild board hunts and collection jobs ask for what lives around the town. Conformance suite `fauna`; the guild vectors and the world parameters change for every client.
- M4d (part): the journal and the language setting. `Q` (desktop) or the Journal button (Android) opens the journal with the quests in progress (text and counts) and the finished ones, board quests included; `F2` or the EN/IT button switches between Italian and English at any time and the choice is kept in `settings.json` (specs 07, 16).
- M4c: the guild board (spec 07, "Guild"). The guild clerk of a town opens a board with new work every day: kill and collect jobs generated from the seed, the town and the day (reward and difficulty grow with the slot), accepted with E/Enter or a tap. Ranks F to S unlock harder slots and are earned with guild points (the slime quest gives 30); generated quests are saved by id and rebuilt on load, and the tracker shows their counts and place. Conformance suite `guild` and scenario `board_quest_rewards`.
- M6b: equipment and shops (spec 06). More items (iron and steel swords, leather vest, chain mail, small potion); weapon and armor slots that change attack and defense (`E` in the bag wears them, worn ones are marked); every region has its own shop (prices and goods differ across the world) that opens from the shopkeeper's dialogue: buy and sell with gold, Tab switches between them. Equipment is saved. Shared combat scenarios `equip_changes_stats` and `shop_buy_and_sell`.
- M5g: settlement people (specs 07, 09, 12). Every hamlet, village and town has people (elder, shopkeeper, innkeeper, guild clerk, villagers) with a generated name and look, who appear and disappear with the world around you. Each region has its own voice (warm, mystic, gruff, flowery, stoic, salty, grim, scholarly) and each place its own quirk, so the same role talks differently in every part of the world: 40 role dialogues and 12 quirks in Italian and English (`tools/make_role_dialogues.py`). Guild clerks of towns offer the slime quest. Dialogue texts take arguments ({npc}, {place}, {region}, {quirk}), six new character palettes, names above the NPC in range, conformance of the people in `worldgen_chunks`.
- A home village next to the start (spec 09): a 72 tile meadow of plains around the spawn and a village of fixed position about 50 tiles to the south-east, so the first settlement is a short walk away; the other settlements are still placed from the seed.
- M5f: regions and settlements (spec 09). Eight named regions (the name is shown on the HUD and on the map); hamlets, villages and towns placed from the seed (about 130 in the world), drawn by stamps with walls, houses, plazas, wells, lamps and roads; no random trees, enemies or rivers inside them; their names are generated from syllable tables and shown on the world map. New tiles (plank floor, cobblestones), props (well, lamp, barrel), `schemas/stamp.schema.json`, `tools/make_stamps.py`, conformance suite `settlements`, and the vectors of `worldgen_chunks` include chunks with settlements.
- M6a: items can be used (spec 06). The bag has a cursor (W/S or arrows, D-pad, or tapping a row), shows the description of the selected item and uses it with E, Enter or X (or a second tap); bread heals 30 health, and using an item that would change nothing is refused; shared combat scenarios `use_bread_heals` and `use_refused_at_full_health`. Esc closes the map or bag before it quits the game.
- M5e: exploration and the world map (spec 09). The cells of 128 x 128 tiles around the player become explored and are kept in saves; press `M` (or the map button on Android) to see the explored world by biome with your position; conformance suite `worldmap`.
- M5d: rivers and roads (spec 09). Winding rivers with fords every few tens of tiles, roads, and bridges where they meet; new tiles (shallows, road, bridge); no props or enemy spawners on them; the world generation vectors change for every client.
- M5c: world time (spec 08). A 20 minute day with a night that darkens the screen, weather (clear, cloudy, rain) that is a function of the seed and the time, a clock on the HUD, and enemies that respawn 10 minutes after their death when the player comes back to their chunk. `world_ticks` and the time of each death are kept in saves; conformance suite `worldtime` and respawn steps in `chunk_window`.
- M5b-2: the world is streamed. The overworld is now 16384 x 16384 tiles and only the 5 x 5 chunks around the player exist: collision, drawing and enemies come from the loaded chunks, killed enemies stay dead, saves keep the cleared spawners and the enemies of the window (spec 09 "Streaming", spec 12); conformance suite `chunk_window` and a streamed-world test on both clients.
- M5b-1: world generation by chunks (spec 09, 32 x 32 tiles) for worlds up to 65536 x 65536 tiles; `generate_chunk` in the reference, C++ and Kotlin, conformance suite `worldgen_chunks`, and a test that chunks equal the whole-world generation. The game does not stream chunks yet.
- M5a: procedural open world (spec 09). A 512 x 512 overworld generated from a seed with integer-only noise: sea, beaches, plains, forests, deserts, snow and mountains, with props, enemy spawners and the guild clerk at the spawn clearing; parameters in `data/base/worlds/overworld.json`; the seed is kept in the save so the same world comes back; conformance suite `worldgen` run by both clients; new tiles and props (sand, snow, mountain, forest floor, pine, cactus); `--map=demo|world` and `--seed=` on desktop.
- M4b: save files (spec 12 v1) readable by both clients and by the Python reference, with the resume suite `save`; autosave every 30 s and at exit, quick save/load (F5/F9) and new game (F7) on desktop, autosave on pause on Android; starting inventory and a bag screen (I); notices.
- M4a: quests, dialogue, conditions and effects (spec 07). The guild clerk Mira offers the slime quest; NPCs, interaction (E, gamepad X, touch button), dialogue box with choices, quest tracker and gold on both clients; shared `story` scenarios.
- M3: real-time combat slice. Sword swing with an arc hitbox, slimes that chase, strike and return, damage numbers, XP and level-ups, loot, a HUD, 12 slimes on the demo map; shared combat scenarios (suite `combat`) run by both clients; spec 05.
- M2: in-house vector rasterizer, rigs and clips, tile map (Tiled TMJ), player movement and collisions, camera, demo map and art; the desktop (SDL3) and Android (libGDX) clients draw and move a vector character; conformance suites rig_pose, movement and raster; specs 02, 03, 13, 16; ADR 0012.
- M1: spec 00, 01, 04 (formulas), 12 (draft) and 17; Python reference (RNG, hash, noise, stats, damage, XP, message format, IDs); conformance vectors; JSON schemas and sample content; pack loader and the same numerics in C++ and Kotlin, both running the shared vectors.
- M0: repository foundations (docs, ADRs, schemas, data validation, CI workflows, desktop and Android skeletons).
